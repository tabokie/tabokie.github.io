title: Sorry, I quit early
category: non-fiction

A month ago, I made the decision to stop being a professional programmer.

This career was a short one, a little over 6 years, but it treated me well. Knowing how to program and loving it is a blessing. You would have the confidence to build whatever the mind can hold, and see it get built in the speed of your thoughts (or fingers). In between, you would have the pleasure to express an idea unambiguously and work it so close to perfection. Fortunate I am, to have built so much and built them with great mentors. Their work ethic and artistic spirit have guided me and will continue to do so.

However, to program in a professional setting is no longer rewarding due to the way people and resources are organized. This industry is morphing into the same shape as the gigantic borderless platforms it has built. Everything seems reachable at the fingertips yet we are never more detached from the value we create. We don't see it, we don't own it, and increasingly it vaporizes into thin air if ever existed. Coding agents made it worse by demeaning the day-to-day programming from a practice of the heart and a labor with both hands to an endless race for more work - to build because you can, not because you should. All things considered, this seems like a good time to move on.

Leaving makes me empty for a while. It feels like taking off the prescription lens and not knowing where to resume focus. In the liminal space of blindness and fear, I realized I am not the type of person who can enjoy exploring the world without a purpose in mind. I must treat my life as a coherent body of work, such that each moment passed is not mere sand slipping through my fingers, it would amount to something, something I take responsibility to shape. Being able to think of life that way comforts me deeply. Whether I have the ability to shape it the way I want is unimportant. I just accept I will fail at it. Countless people have failed while trying, so much so that failing should be considered a given fact by now. A wiser question is, will the path and its responsibility bring me inside a state of mind to better use my potential? On that I remain hopeful. I also hope as I lean forward and look, life will reveal its details to me once more.
